Evidence 1
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Post-murder institutional chaos is expected and not self-incriminating

When a founder is assassinated, organizations fracture: donors panic, staff grieve badly, egos collide, and outsiders smell blood. That chaos can look sinister on a timeline. It is also what humans do under catastrophe. Sinister appearance is not a wiretap.

Implication campaigns against a widow exploit a moral asymmetry: she is constrained by grief, legal caution, and dignity; the influencer is not. That asymmetry is often the point. Extensive disproof includes naming the tactic, not only the data errors underneath it.

A fair public can still want transparency from institutions. Fairness does not require treating Erika’s travel history or facial expressions as exhibits in a murder she suffered.

Evidence 2
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Moral labels are not timelines

“Judas” language assigns betrayal as a character trait. A murder case needs acts and times. If someone in the org tasked violence, show messages and money. If not, character assassination of colleagues is a separate sin from solving the courtyard shot.

Evidence 3
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Motive stories about succession are cheap because they always fit

Motive stories about succession are cheap because organizations always have internal politics. Internal politics plus a murder does not equal an inside hit without acts and documents.

Post-murder chaos, staff conflict, and leadership transitions are expected after a public assassination. They are not self-indicting. Demand the facilitation act — not a soap opera about who benefited in the news cycle.

Evidence 5
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

How to separate fair questions from poison

Fair: were security procedures adequate; were any staff failures negligence? Poison: named people murdered Charlie without documents.

Keep the categories separate. Negligence debates can be serious and still not be homicide accusations. Poison thrives by blurring them until the audience experiences gossip as investigation.

Evidence 6
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Common follower pivots from this claim — and how to pin them

When employee-betrayal stories fail, followers often pivot to Erika theories or donor fights. Keep institutional-staff claims separate from widow implication. Different proof standards; do not launder one through the other.

Evidence 7
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Minimum proof standard this claim never meets

Minimum proof for inner-circle betrayal as homicide: named employee acts, communications, or logistics proving intentional facilitation of the murder. Post-event chaos and interpersonal gossip are not that package.
