Evidence 1
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Logical dependence on the exploding-mic claim

This entire branch is a conditional: IF the mic was a bomb, THEN the last tech matters. The IF fails under gunshot video and firearm exhibits. Therefore the THEN is ordinary staffing trivia, not a murder tree.

Exotic device claims borrow prestige from spy cinema. The prestige is not evidence. Device claims die or live on recovery, lab ID, and medical correlation. Internet teardowns and brand recognition are stage props that make a theory feel “technical” to non-engineers.

If a follower says “you’re not a bomb expert either,” agree — then note that the burden is still on the person asserting a secret bomb. Skepticism of a bomb claim does not require you to be a demolitions tech. It requires the claimant to meet a normal proof standard.

Evidence 2
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Live events have many hands on audio

Contractors, volunteers, staff, and venue people touch gear. Building a public suspect gallery from that fact without device forensics is how bystanders get destroyed for episodes.

Outdoor political events are audio-intensive: redundant mics, rapid swaps, and multiple vendors. Ordinary handoffs look sinister only after you have already decided the pack was a weapon. Without residue, device reconstruction, or blast-trauma findings, “who last touched the mic” is event staffing — not a murder tree.

Evidence 3
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Brand presence is not bomb presence

A RODE product on site means a brand was used. It does not mean the unit contained a charge. Conflating brand with bomb is the core sleight.

Wireless mics, lav packs, and handheld units are ordinary event gear at political rallies. Finding the same brand in a teardown video or a product photo does not identify energetic material, a detonator, or a fatal blast pattern on Kirk’s clothing. Brand recognition is marketing literacy, not explosive ordnance disposal. Until a specific unit is recovered, lab-identified, and medically correlated to the wound, “RODE was there” is logistics trivia dressed as forensics.

Evidence 4
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Runtime expansion is not evidence expansion

More episodes about the mic tech create more content hours, not more proof of explosion. Measure proof, not episode count.

Dependent subplots thrive on runtime: each new name, badge photo, or staffing oddity feels like progress while the parent mechanism (mic-as-bomb) remains unproven. That is how a collapsed foundation still generates seasons of content. When the IF fails, the THEN collapses with it — no matter how many hours the show spends on the AV tree.
