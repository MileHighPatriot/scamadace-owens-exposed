1 Her claim — what she said

After the July 2026 preliminary hearing, Candace Owens returned from break arguing that an ATF forensic biologist admitted it was “scientifically indefensible” to claim Tyler’s DNA was on the gun, that his DNA was the most degraded among contributors, and that bullet fragments were too small for definitive ballistics. Clips titled around “ATF Called Media Headlines Indefensible” flooded her orbit.

What the public court record actually shows: analysts testified mixed DNA profiles exist; Robinson is the major contributor on multiple rifle areas; trigger/trigger-guard likelihood ratios were reported at least one trillion to one favoring inclusion; towel DNA tied to Robinson and roommate Lance Twiggs. Defense cross-examination properly attacked absolute language and mixture interpretation—the expert agreeing that crude media headlines are not scientifically careful is not the same as “no DNA link.”

Owens converts careful forensic pedantry into audience-facing exoneration. That is the opposite of what the numbers presented in open court say.

Evidence 1
Tier 1 — Court / charging / official case facts

What the ATF witness actually supported in open court

AP, CNN, and Utah outlets reported ATF DNA section testimony that samples from parts of the rifle were at least 1 trillion times more likely contributed by Robinson than not — the highest statistic permitted under lab guidelines. Coverage also described DNA support on the towel wrapped around the gun and a screwdriver found on the Losee Center roof.

Cross-examination produced concessions about mixtures and about media shorthand. That is ordinary capital-case DNA litigation. It is not a laboratory confession that the case is a frame. Owens’s audience hears “indefensible” and concludes “no DNA.” The stats say major-contributor inclusion.

Evidence 2
Tier 2 — Named officials & law-enforcement briefings

Mixed DNA and degradation are normal, not exonerating

Rifles move through multiple hands; recovery environments degrade DNA. “Most degraded” is not “never touched.” Twiggs DNA on a household towel is expected if the towel came from a shared home — not proof of a federal plant.

Mixture interpretation is hard science done carefully. Turning that care into a podcast “gotcha” is how you launder defense cross into a total innocence narrative without answering the likelihood ratios.

Sources

  • Guardian: DNA and rooftop evidence at hearing
Evidence 3
Tier 1 — Court / charging / official case facts

Inconclusive jacket comparison ≠ “no gun” and ≠ “mic bomb”

Hearing coverage also noted an ATF finding that a bullet jacket fragment from Kirk’s body could not be identified or excluded as fired from the recovered rifle — inconclusive microscopic marks. Defense highlighted this. Prosecutors still rely on the broader package: DNA, surveillance, note, texts, engraving chain (Dremel / “TEST SHOT” casings).

Owens’s audience hears “exoneration.” Science says “null on that one test.” An inconclusive jacket comparison does not resurrect an exploding microphone.

Evidence 4
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

How to pin the “scientifically indefensible” clip

Ask three questions: (1) Did the expert give likelihood ratios including Robinson as major contributor? (2) Did cross attack absolute media wording or wipe the stats? (3) What competing lab report has Owens produced?

If (1) is yes and (3) is nothing, the clip is spin. Defense work at a preliminary hearing is not the same as scientific collapse of the case. Courts allow fights over mixture interpretation precisely because DNA can be powerful and still contested — contested is not empty.
