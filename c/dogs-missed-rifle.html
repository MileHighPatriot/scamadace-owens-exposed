Bomb dogs “failed” / Freya had a “bad sniffer,” so the rifle find is staged (and Robinson’s “pooch” text proves the setup)

1 Her claim — what she said

On Ep 367 Owens clarified earlier online confusion: some dogs chased a human scent near the amphitheater (she nicknames Loki), while bomb dog Freya searched a field and “came up empty” until federal direction sent rookie officers back and the gun appeared. She reads Robinson’s alleged text to Lance Twiggs — hoping the “pooch has a bad sniffer” — as uncanny foreshadowing that “sounds like someone’s new to the English language,” i.e., fabricated Fed writing.

The misleading move is converting ordinary K9 limits and a second search into proof of planting. Dogs miss. Handlers re-search. Rookies find evidence. None of that requires a Hollywood prop rifle. Treating a suspect’s text about dogs as proof the text is fake is circular: if he dumped a gun, worrying about dogs is exactly what a guilty person texts.

Evidence 1
Tier 4 — Multi-outlet reporting citing documents

K9 non-alert ≠ empty field forever

Bomb dogs and trailing dogs have different training, wind, contamination, and handler cueing issues. A non-alert followed by a visual search finding a wrapped or distant rifle is not a logical proof of planting. It is a reason for humans to keep looking.

Evidence 2
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

“The text is too on-the-nose” is not authentication science

Owens has repeatedly called confessional communications Fed theater. Authentication is for labs and phone extractions, not for whether a phrase sounds Gen-Z enough for her ear. Irony: she simultaneously needs the text to be “real enough” to mock and “fake enough” to free Robinson.

Evidence 3
Tier 1 — Court / charging / official case facts

Weapon recovery path is secondary to weapon-to-suspect science in public court story

Even if first-pass dogs missed, prosecutors still have to connect the recovered rifle to Robinson via DNA, prints, ownership trail, and video. The “dogs failed therefore plant” slogan skips those exhibits.
