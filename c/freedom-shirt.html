Evidence 1
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Personal effects scatter in medical emergencies

Clothing can be cut, bagged, discarded, or lost without a conspiracy. Each path has a paperwork test. Global sinister readings skip the paperwork.

Trauma teams cut shirts to reach wounds. Transport and hospital intake generate bags, tags, and sometimes incomplete inventories under chaos. Those facts create discrepancies that internet sleuths treat as scrubbing. The adult version is chain-of-custody requests for specific items — not treating every missing garment narrative as proof the mic exploded.

Cover-up theories must be itemized. “They destroyed everything” is almost always false as a global statement and unfalsifiable as a slogan. Itemization sounds like: exhibit X was unavailable because Y, and that blocks test Z. If the speaker cannot name X, Y, and Z, they are performing atmosphere.

Also watch the dual rule: missing items prove scrubbing; present items prove planting. Dual rules are for keeping a narrative immortal. Reject immortality devices.

Evidence 2
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Discrepancy mining needs a link to the mechanism

A shirt mystery that never connects to who fired and how is atmospheric content. Useful investigation requires the link.

Even a fully documented clothing path would still leave the core questions: rifle vs. device, rooftop vs. alternate origin, charged defendant vs. foreign cell. Clothing follow-ups that never touch those questions are filler that keeps an audience in investigative mood without advancing proof.

Evidence 3
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Privacy and dignity also explain limited public detail

Families and teams do not owe internet sleuths a full costume inventory. Absence of public shirt narrative is not proof of scrubbing.

Grief, legal caution, and basic dignity constrain what the Kirk family and counsel release. Treating their silence on wardrobe trivia as evidence of a cover-up weaponizes normal privacy. Demand public exhibits that matter to mechanism and identity; do not demand a widow’s clothing spreadsheet for content.

Evidence 4
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

How to answer “what happened to the shirt?”

Maybe cut in care, maybe bagged, maybe not fully public — show records if you assert scrubbing. Until then, it is not a pillar of the murder theory.

Force the claimant to state what the shirt would prove if found. If the answer is only “it would feel satisfying,” the claim is aesthetic. If they allege explosive residue or blast pattern on fabric, that is a lab claim — and still subordinate to the multi-angle gunshot record and firearm exhibits already in the public case.
