1 Her claim — what she said

Candace Owens has repeatedly focused on Charlie Kirk’s chief of staff Mikey McCoy as a figure of suspicion: viral video of him on a phone call around the shooting, disputes over whether he had “blood all over him,” claims about who he called first, and allegations that TPUSA soft-sold him as a successor. Politico notes she dialed in on McCoy and camera operator Terryl Farnsworth early.

Chaotic VIP assassinations produce ugly optics. Staff freeze, call the wrong person first, film when they should help, or look composed when audiences want visible panic. Optics are not wiretaps. Converting McCoy’s crisis behavior into complicity requires tasking, money, or acts connected to the shot — none of which appear in the public charging case against Robinson.

This page separates legitimate after-action questions (security, communications) from homicide accusation by innuendo.

Evidence 1
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Crisis optics ≠ actus reus

A phone call during a mass-casualty VIP event is expected. Security, family, legal, and organizational trees light up in seconds. Looking “wrong” on a cropped video is not proof of prior knowledge.

Standard for complicity: order, payment, delivery of the shooter, or obstruction with intent to hide the true killer. Viral body language analysis fails that standard.

Evidence 2
Tier 1 — Court / charging / official case facts

Charged defendant is still Robinson

Open proceedings and charging presentations publicly build a case against Tyler Robinson with rifle DNA, surveillance, and communications. They do not charge Mikey McCoy as a co-conspirator. Owens’s audience is invited to try McCoy in the court of content without the exhibits that would be required in a real court.

Evidence 3
Tier 4 — Multi-outlet reporting citing documents

Disputes over early narrative details are not a murder plot

Organizations botch first-day storytelling under trauma and media siege. Contradictions about blood, call order, or who is called a “hero” can be corrected without implying the staff ran a hit.

Demand primary video timestamps and contemporaneous messages if alleging a specific lie tied to the shot. Do not treat every PR error as conspiracy fuel.
