1 Her claim — what she said

In early August 2026, Candace Owens aired a segment (also pushed on Facebook/social) in which she said she combed through footage sent to her on September 10 and found who Officer Bagley was with on a Losee rooftop around 12:44–12:47 p.m. She framed it as doing what “all the resources of the feds and UVU police and all the cameras” could not do before the preliminary hearing — complete with a sarcastic “Oh my gosh, you’re welcome, Officer Bagley” performance while zooming stills of a “plain clothes” figure and a red-hat silhouette.

Within days, critics (including a viral Cinema Shogun post covered in secondary press) accused her of presenting inaccurate information and walking back false allegations against the officer in a later episode. Whether every specific name/ID in the segment was later softened is secondary to the method: treat ordinary scene-policing ambiguity as a gotcha that reopens the murder mechanism, and cast herself as the only competent investigator.

Identifying two people on a roof after a mass-casualty event — if accurate — is not proof of a second shooter, a mic bomb, or a frame-up of Robinson. First responders and plainclothes personnel on elevated positions are expected. The leap from “here is a still” to “authorities hid the real plot” is the unsupported part.

Evidence 1
Tier 5 — Analysis / logic (clearly labeled)

Scene officers on roofs ≠ assassin plot

After a public assassination, law enforcement occupies high ground, secures buildings, and sweeps for secondary threats. Showing an officer (or companion) on a roof at 12:44–12:47 — after the shot window discussed in court coverage — does not overturn the charged shooter’s identity package. Time stamps matter: post-event security presence is not pre-event sniper proof.
